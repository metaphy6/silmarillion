## Akallabêth completion

- Pages 338-340: Pharazôn takes the throne through a coerced marriage; overwhelming military strength obtains Sauron's submission but creates a route for his political influence. Strategic implication: capturing an adversary is not the same as ending his agency, and access to counsel is a form of power.
- Pages 340-343: court influence becomes persecution, cult and extraction. Wealth and military capacity grow while freedom, happiness and security deteriorate. Do not begin the entire decline with Sauron's arrival: the preceding chapter portion establishes older imperial growth and estrangement.
- Page 341: Isildur's rescue of Nimloth's fruit is an unusually strong example of saving a future through a small, dangerous action. The object is living continuity rather than a generic resource pickup.
- Pages 343-344: Amandil prepares an appeal while Elendil prepares ships. His ultimate fate remains unknown. Preparation preserves people, lore, skilled work and meaningful possessions without proving that every intervention succeeds.
- Pages 345-349: portents become political evidence interpreted to fit an existing commitment. The king briefly hesitates at the destination but proceeds. A system about warning should distinguish receiving information from being willing to change course.
- Pages 349-354: rescue and exile coexist; geography changes. Later longing for the West does not receive an ordinary navigable solution. A faithful game should not turn the Straight Road into a standard unlockable shipping lane.

## Of the Rings of Power and the Third Age

- Pages 357-362: Sauron's attempted repentance, refusal of judgment, renewed pride and attractive gifts connect shame, power and dependency. Eregion's desire to preserve and improve is real; the trap exploits that desire. The Three remain dependent on the One despite being made without Sauron's touch.
- Pages 363-369: settlement, communication, coalition, costly victory, inherited claims and overconfidence shape the new kingdoms. The palantíri function through connected observation, not simply omniscience. Isildur's claim to the Ring is framed as compensation for loss, repeating the broader theme of possession after grief.
- Pages 370-374: refuges preserve memory; counsel can be delayed by concealed private interests. Saruman's willingness to postpone action serves his own Ring-seeking. Information sharing and conflicts of interest make institutional design consequential.
- Pages 375-378: the small actors overlooked by powerful planners decide the outcome. Victory ends the form of preservation sustained by the Three; departure accompanies renewal. This supports endings measured by what becomes possible, not only what remains unchanged.

## Reference material and evidence limits

The index distinguishes otherwise easily confused people and places: the two Minas Tiriths, two Míriels, two Celeborns, different Eärendurs and Gelmirs, and multiple names belonging to one person. Preserve canonical identifiers in production data while giving players clear roles and contextual aliases. Its descriptions are not exhaustive biographies.

The pronunciation guide and name-elements appendix help with text/audio direction. The appendix itself qualifies the apparent certainty of its condensed etymologies (429); it is not a grammar for generating unlimited supposedly authentic names.

The source presents some claims as attributed belief. Amandil's fate (344) and the continued summit of Meneltarma (353) should remain qualified. The note on page 458 explicitly marks a change in king numbering since the Waldman letter. Keep explanatory paratext distinct from the compiled narrative.

The final recommendation is in `silmarillion-game-report.md`. Earlier readers' alternative campaign suggestions are exploratory notes, superseded by that report's narrowly dated Brethil proposal.
